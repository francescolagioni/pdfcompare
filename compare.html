Alternatives

Without uploading them

Most of the convenient options need your document to leave your computer. If that is not acceptable, the field narrows fast. Here is the honest state of it.

The web tools

iLovePDF, Xodo, Draftable and the rest do a decent job and cost nothing. They also require you to upload both versions to a server you do not control. For a published document that is fine. For a draft contract, an unfiled submission or an internal procedure it is usually a breach of your own confidentiality rules — and it is the kind of breach that happens quietly, because uploading a file feels like nothing.

If your document is public, use them. This page is for when it isn't.

Preview on its own

macOS has no built-in comparison. Preview shows two documents side by side, which means comparing them by eye. On a five-page memo that works. On a revised guideline it does not, and the changes you miss are the ones nobody flagged.

What the offline options cost

A comparison tool is something you reach for a few times a month. Paying monthly for a suite you otherwise don't open is a bad trade.

What the cheap options don't do

  • Compares pages with no text layer by aligning the two versions visually — scanned and re-scanned documents included.
  • Matches pages by content, so an inserted page is reported as inserted instead of shifting everything after it.
  • Exports the comparison as a document you can attach to a record.
  • Lets you exclude regions — headers, footers, revision stamps — that change on every page and drown the real differences.

And what this app doesn't do

It does not edit, sign, convert or merge PDFs. It does no OCR, so a scanned page is compared as an image rather than as text. Two documents at a time, not folders. macOS 14 and later, no Windows version.

If you need any of those, a full editor is the right purchase and this app is not.
